Getting the numbers in

LinkedIn does not let tools read a page's views and clicks without special approved access, so there are three ways to bring the numbers in:

  1. Screenshot. Open the post's analytics in LinkedIn, take a screenshot, and let the AI read the numbers off it.

  3. Tracked link. Each post has a link with campaign tags. When people click it, your Google Analytics shows website visits from that exact post (Reports, Acquisition, Traffic acquisition, then look for the campaign content-studio).

Quick start

  1. Dashboard. Shows where you are this week and suggests the next step.
  2. Studio → Find latest topics. Runs one research search of trade press and official sources, about 15 seconds. Each click covers the next area (events and meetups, tech news, then community and learning) and adds new ideas to the list. Search all areas runs all three at once at three times the cost. A search already made today is reused for free.
  3. Pick three ideas. Use Auto-fill week, or press Mon / Wed / Fri on any idea. ★ marks the day the research suggests.
  4. Write my week. Writes three posts and checks each one against its sources.
  5. Create image, then Post to LinkedIn. Review the text, make the image, and send it to LinkedIn's composer. You press Post yourself.

Build a post from your own article or screenshot

Found something relevant? In Studio, press From a link or screenshot.

  1. Paste the article's link, or add a screenshot (choose a file, or paste one with Cmd+V).
  2. Optionally say what angle you want, for example "what it means for first-time attendees".
  3. Choose Monday, Wednesday or Friday, or "Just add to my ideas".
  4. Press Read it and build the post. The studio reads the page, pulls out the facts, and writes and fact-checks that day's post.
  • Only facts that are really in the page or screenshot are used. For links, any figure that is not on the page is thrown away.
  • Some publishers block automatic reading or hide text behind a paywall. If a link fails, take a screenshot instead.
  • Posts from a screenshot are marked From your screenshot: check the figures against the original before posting.

Dragging files in

Drag files from your computer onto any page and drop them. Several at once is fine. A message says where each one went.

  • Photos are saved to the Library. Drop one onto a post's image area instead and it becomes that post's picture.
  • Documents (PDF, Word, text) are attached to the Assistant chat, up to two. Dropped anywhere else, the first one becomes a post idea.
  • On the Analytics page, one screenshot of a post's analytics fills in the numbers.
  • Other file types are skipped. The Choose files buttons still work.

Using your own photos

  • Open Library and add your real photographs (JPEG, PNG, WebP or iPhone HEIC). The AI names, describes and tags each photo for you, and flags any that show people so you can check consent. Anything you type yourself is kept, and Describe with AI redoes a photo. Tick Cleared for public use only when you have confirmed permission for the photo and everyone in it.
  • On any post, press Use a library photo. The headline and logo are drawn on top of the real photo, with no AI involved. Use Position and Zoom to frame it.
  • To change the photo, type an edit such as "make the sky clearer" and press Edit photo. The AI is told to keep the equipment, people and layout exactly as photographed, and the post is marked edited with AI so you check it. Revert to original undoes it.
  • To build a new scene around a photo, press Upload your own photo (or choose one from the Library), describe the setting, for example "in a London co-working space at dusk, laptops open", and press Generate around photo. The subject stays as photographed and the AI creates the surroundings in the post format. Check the subject against the original before publishing.
  • Photos stay in your browser only; they are not uploaded to a server.

Analytics: views and clicks per post

LinkedIn does not let tools read a page's analytics without special approved access, so the Analytics tab works from numbers you bring in:

  1. Mark a post as posted in Studio (it appears in Analytics automatically), or press Track a post.
  2. Open that post's analytics in LinkedIn, take a screenshot, and upload it. The AI copies only the numbers that are visible; check them, then Save. You can also type the numbers in.
  3. Each post gets views, clicks, click-through, reactions, comments, reposts and an engagement rate, compared with your other posts.
  4. Tick Add a tracked link on a post, or copy its tracked link from Analytics. When people click it, your Google Analytics shows website visits from that exact post (look for the campaign content-studio).

The charts and "What the numbers say" fill in once you have two or more posts. Judge trends after 8 to 12 posts.

The Assistant: just say what you want

Use the Assistant when the post is not news: a greeting, a thank-you, a seasonal post, an announcement, or a post made from your own notes. Write it the way you would tell a colleague. You can also press Attach document to add a PDF or Word file (a guide, procedure or brochure) and ask for a post from it. Internal records such as delivery orders, quotes, forms and back-office procedures are refused on purpose, because they hold customer details.

We're looking to create an image post for Halloween to wish our people a happy Halloween.
Thank our customers for a busy year. Keep it warm but short.
We've opened a new meetup in Shoreditch on 3 November. Announce it. (Give it the facts.)
Turn these notes into a post: [paste your notes]
  • It writes and briefs the image together. You get the post text, a headline for the picture, and a checklist of what the image must and must not show.
  • Change it by talking. Say "shorter", "mention our Irish office", "make the picture more autumn". It keeps what you did not ask to change.
  • It may ask one question. For announcements it needs real details such as place and date. It will not invent them.
  • It will not make up news or figures. For market news use Studio → Find latest topics, or paste the facts into the chat.
  • Create image costs a few cents and takes under two minutes. If something is wrong, describe it under the image and press Fix and remake.

The weekly Studio

  • Ideas come with sources. Open "source facts" to see exactly what each post will be allowed to say.
  • Add your own lets you enter facts you hold, one per line, with where they come from. Only those facts are used. You can also upload a PDF or Word document: the AI reads it, picks the best facts for a post, and fills them in for you to check and edit before choosing a day.
  • Edit the text directly in the box; the word count shows the target of 120 to 200 words.
  • Change the theme or tone under each post: pick Longer, Shorter, What it means for members, More practical, Warmer, More formal or Ask the community, or describe the change. It is fact-checked again, and Undo last revision goes back.
  • Write it again rewrites one day. Mark as posted updates the dashboard.
  • Image brief used shows the checklist the image was made from. Tick Research images online in the sidebar to have it checked against how the real thing looks (it costs extra).
  • Each new image is shot from a different angle and in different light, and avoids repeating the week's other images.
  • Posts and ideas are kept in your browser, so they are still there after a refresh. Images are not kept: download them.

How accuracy works

Nothing is written from memory. A post may only say what its source facts say. After writing, a second check reads the post line by line against those facts and flags anything extra.

Images are AI-generated illustrations. They are realistic but not real photographs of your operations. Have someone check equipment details before publishing, and prefer real your photos for real events.

Posting to LinkedIn

  1. Press Post to LinkedIn on a post. The image is copied and downloaded, and LinkedIn's composer opens with your text filled in.
  2. Click in the LinkedIn post box and press Cmd+V (Ctrl+V on Windows) to attach the image. If nothing attaches, use the photo icon and choose the downloaded file.
  3. Read it over and press Post yourself. The Studio never publishes anything on its own.
  4. Post as the your company page using a page-admin role. Never share personal LinkedIn passwords with any tool.

Setup and settings

For whoever installs it. Full details are in README.md.

  • Copy .env.example to .env and put your OpenAI key in .env, never in .env.example. Restart the server after changes.
  • OPENAI_RESEARCH_MODEL (default gpt-4.1-mini) controls research and reading. OPENAI_WRITER_MODEL (default gpt-4.1) writes each post's first draft. OPENAI_MODEL (default gpt-4.1-mini) runs the fact-checks and the Assistant. OPENAI_IMAGE_MODEL and OPENAI_IMAGE_QUALITY (default low) control images.
  • In the sidebar, Image quality switches between Draft (cheapest) and Final, and the line below it counts this session's calls, tokens, searches and images. OpenAI's own usage page has the true spend.
  • The older version of the app is still available under "Classic view" in the sidebar.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
"No OpenAI key" in the sidebarAdd OPENAI_API_KEY to .env and restart.
Page says it can't be reachedThe server has stopped. Start it again with npm start and use the address it prints.
Very few topics foundPress Find latest topics again, or use Add your own with facts you hold.
Image paste does nothing in LinkedInUse the photo icon in LinkedIn and pick the downloaded file.
"Search did not run" style errorsThe account or model may not allow web search. Check OPENAI_RESEARCH_MODEL.
